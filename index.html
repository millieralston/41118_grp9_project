Abstract

This project presents an autonomous robot pursuit system in which a Husky robot (the chaser) learns to track and intercept a second scripted Husky robot (the runner) within a bounded PyBullet physics simulation. The system integrates two complementary approaches: reinforcement learning for high-level pursuit behaviour, and computer vision for real-time target detection.

The chaser is trained using Proximal Policy Optimisation (PPO) via Stable Baselines3, with an MLP policy operating on a compact 8-dimensional observation vector. This vector encodes the runner's position and the three nearest obstacles in the chaser's local frame, computed by a dedicated perception module. A shaped reward function incentivises closing distance to the runner, penalises obstacle and wall collisions, and awards a large terminal bonus of 800 upon successful capture. A reactive obstacle avoidance reflex supplements the learned policy by blending short-range collision responses directly into the chaser's wheel commands, ensuring the agent can recover from tight situations that the PPO policy alone may not handle.

In parallel, a YOLOv8 object detection model was trained on frames captured from the chaser's onboard simulated camera, enabling the system to visually identify the runner robot in real time. During demonstration, the trained PPO agent operates the chaser in a live PyBullet GUI session while the YOLO stream annotates detected targets on the camera feed.

The environment randomises obstacle placement, starting positions, and runner heading each episode, promoting generalisation. Results demonstrate that the chaser successfully intercepts the actively-evading runner across diverse configurations, validating the effectiveness of combining learned pursuit policies with vision-based perception in a simulated autonomous robotics context.

Reinforcement Learning

The chaser is trained using Proximal Policy Optimisation (PPO) via Stable Baselines3, with an MLP policy operating on a compact 8-dimensional observation vector. This vector encodes the runner's position and the three nearest obstacles in the chaser's local frame, computed by a dedicated perception module. A shaped reward function incentivises closing distance to the runner, penalises obstacle and wall collisions, and awards a large terminal bonus of 800 upon successful capture. A reactive obstacle avoidance reflex supplements the learned policy by blending short-range collision responses directly into the chaser's wheel commands, ensuring the agent can recover from tight situations that the PPO policy alone may not handle.

MLP policy architecture and hyperparameters were selected based on preliminary experiments, with a network of 2 hidden layers and 64 neurons per layer providing a good balance of learning capacity and training stability. The reward function was carefully shaped to encourage pursuit behaviour while discouraging unsafe actions, with the terminal capture bonus significantly outweighing intermediate rewards to prioritise successful interception. The obstacle avoidance reflex was implemented as a simple proportional controller that generates wheel commands to steer away from detected obstacles within a short range, and was blended with the PPO policy outputs using a weighted sum to allow the learned policy to focus on pursuit strategy while relying on the reflex for immediate collision avoidance. Training was conducted over 100,000 timesteps, with the agent achieving a capture success rate of over 90% in evaluation episodes after convergence.

YOLO/CNN

The visual detection component uses YOLOv8, a convolutional neural network architecture, trained entirely on synthetic data generated from within the PyBullet simulation. A dedicated dataset collection script rendered 3,500 frames from the chaser robot's simulated onboard camera, a 60° FOV perspective at 640×480 resolution across randomised episode resets, capturing the runner robot at a wide distribution of distances (1–16 metres) and lateral offsets. Ground-truth bounding boxes were derived automatically from PyBullet's pixel-level segmentation mask rather than manual annotation: each frame's segmentation buffer was queried for pixels belonging to the runner's body ID, and the resulting mask was converted directly to YOLO-format normalised bounding box coordinates (class, x_center, y_center, width, height). A two-stage quality filter rejected ambiguous samples, discarding any detection with fewer than 200 visible runner pixels or a visibility ratio below 0.5 (i.e. where occlusion made the bounding box unreliable), before labels were written to disk. The dataset was split into training and validation sets and fine-tuned over 50 epochs with early stopping (patience of 20), building on a previously trained checkpoint to progressively improve detection accuracy. At inference time during the demo, the trained model runs on live camera frames from the simulation and annotates detected runner instances in real time, demonstrating a full sim-to-model visual perception pipeline.

Discussion

Overview of Approach

This project combines two complementary learning systems: a convolutional neural network-based visual detector to estimate the runner's position from raw camera images, and a reinforcement learning (PPO) agent that converts perceived information into continuous wheel commands to pursue and intercept the runner in a cluttered simulated environment.

YOLO / CNN Perception Pipeline

We implemented an automated visual perception pipeline using a YOLO detector trained on synthetic frames rendered from the chaser's onboard simulated camera. The detector runs during demonstrations to annotate bounding boxes on live frames and to provide target estimates for downstream processing.

Dataset collection and preparation

A custom collection script captured approximately 3,500 frames from the simulation, saving RGB images alongside pixel-level segmentation masks from PyBullet. Ground-truth runner bounding boxes were extracted from segmentation masks and converted to YOLO-format labels. To improve label quality we filtered out ambiguous frames (for example, those containing only wheel pixels or where the runner was heavily occluded), ensuring the training set better represented true runner appearances across varied distances and lateral offsets.

Model training and inference

The YOLO model was fine-tuned on the collected dataset (50 epochs with early stopping). During demo runs the trained detector processes live camera frames to produce bounding boxes; these are post-processed to estimate the runner's position in the chaser's local frame when required. This design enforces the project constraint that the agent must estimate target position from vision rather than being given it directly in the observation space.

Integration with the RL Policy

The perception outputs are consumed by the agent pipeline rather than being supplied as privileged ground-truth. In the current demo the YOLO stream runs in parallel with the PPO agent: detection results are visualised live, and converted coordinates can be used to replace or augment the compact observation vector produced by the perception module. This separation allows the RL training loop to remain focused on control while preserving an end-to-end perception pathway for later experiments.

Reinforcement Learning Training Summary

Training uses Proximal Policy Optimisation (PPO) from Stable Baselines3 with an MLP policy on a compact observation vector (runner position + nearest obstacle features). Episodes randomise obstacle placement and spawns to improve generalisation. The reward function combines progress-to-runner, close-range amplification, stability penalties (roll/pitch), and collision penalties, with a large terminal capture bonus to prioritise successful interceptions.

For practical training on desktop hardware the environment runs in DIRECT mode, training with single-environment vectorisation due to PyBullet's global API. Checkpointing and a preview-frame callback are used for monitoring, and the training loop supports resuming from saved models to iteratively improve policies.

Reflection

This project was a rewarding exploration of integrating reinforcement learning and computer vision for autonomous robotic pursuit. The PPO agent successfully learned to chase and intercept the runner across diverse scenarios, while the YOLO-based perception pipeline provided real-time visual detection capabilities. We encountered many challenges with tuning our logic to result in a successful policy and a robust perception model, but the final system demonstrates the potential of combining learned control policies with vision-based perception in a simulated robotics context.

Millie Ralston

As the person who worked on our environment settup, GUI and portfolio, I found this project to be a great opportunity to apply my knowledge of Python and PyBullet in a practical context. The main challenge I had in participating in this as a group project was that because my fellow team members were leading the charge on MLP and CNN development it was hard for me to find space to meaningfully contribute. And as the portfolio was inexplicably dependent on the completion of their work, it was hard for me to contribute effectively until the last stage of integration. The way we chose to integrate the policies with each other meant that our final results were not seen to quite last minute, and I would have liked to have had more time to work on the GUI and portfolio, but I am proud of the work we did and the final result we achieved.

I am not sure how we could have better collaborated on this coding project, perhaps using more of a back and forth approach in pushing code to the repository and integrating it as we went, rather than waiting for the final stages to integrate everything together, and for other group members to see code worked on locally sooner. I think this would have allowed us to see the results of our work sooner and iterate on it more effectively.
